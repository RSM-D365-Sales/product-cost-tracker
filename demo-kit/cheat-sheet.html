Before you walk up

  • Open the link. Both pages start with an empty Item field, so the screen looks bare until you run something. Pick RAW-BLU from the Item lookup and press Run: Average landed cost should read $3.01 across 55 receipts, and the first four grid rows should be EXPECTED purchase orders dated over the next four weeks. Dates follow today's date on their own; there is nothing to reseed.
  • Browser at 100% zoom, window at least 1280 px wide. Below about 1024 px the figures on the right of each FastTab header disappear.
  • Know the cast below. Every vendor, order and figure is Bluestem's demo set, generated by the app. Never describe it as a live tenant or as executed ERP postings.
  • Leave the page on Product cost inquiry with the Item field empty for the next presenter. A browser refresh does that.
Don't press Export to Excel or Export plan to Excel. They drop a CSV into the booth machine's Downloads and put the browser's download bar over the demo. Settings, Help, the search box and the favourite star do nothing, so don't lead anyone to them. Reset clears the item you picked. Pick items from the lookup instead of typing them: a typo returns a red "does not exist" bar. Everything else is safe to click. Nothing saves, and a refresh puts the app back to its opening state.

The story in thirty seconds

Bluestem buys bulk blueberries through Ridgeview Produce Exchange and receives them into the Hart packing house. Three loads bought at the same $2.35 a pound landed at $2.77, $2.66 and $2.90, because freight, pre-cooling, customs and demurrage are allocated order by order, and one of those loads was held at the border. That landed cost stays with the lot. The pint-clamshell runs that consumed those three lots came out at $28.56, $27.48 and $29.86 a case. Today a case calculates at $30.36 against $28.90 on the item record, and a 62,000 lb lot expires tomorrow. The two Hart lines can't pack all of it, which puts $41,678 of fruit at risk until the Holland co-pack line is committed. One cost, followed from the dock to the production plan.

Figures on this sheet were read from the app on 6 October 2026. The order and receipt numbers never change. Averages can move by a cent as the two-year window rolls forward, and batch numbers and dates roll with the calendar, so read those off the screen.

Questions you will get

Is this actually talking to D365?

Not on the show floor. The booth build runs on the app's own data in the browser. The same screens sit behind one provider interface with two live options: standard D365 OData entities through a small Node proxy that holds the Azure AD secret, or a custom X++ service that does the joins and the charge allocation server-side. The production page is seeded only for now; its BOM, route and batch on-hand entities have to be confirmed per environment first.

Why do three receipts at the same price land differently?

Charge allocation. F&SC stores a header charge once against the order. The inquiry spreads it across every line by the order's allocation method (net amount, quantity, net weight or equally), then prorates by how much of the line each receipt covered. Expand any row for the breakdown.

Is the trend line a forecast?

No, and the screen says so. It is a least-squares line with its r² shown beside it, and the shaded band is a 95% interval for a single future receipt. On an item with a hard seasonal cycle, such as RAW-CUC, the fit drops to a few percent and the message tells you not to quote it.

37 of 59 outside tolerance sounds like a lot.

The baseline is the whole result, and this one spans two years, so inflation alone pushes the oldest and newest receipts out. Open the parameters, set Days back to 90 and run again to compare each receipt with its own season.

How does impact analysis decide?

It nets unexpired stock on hand plus open POs against the planned production pegged to them, first-expired-first-out, day by day. It is modelled on the Procurement agent's impact analysis in D365 Supply Chain Management. The what-if recalculates as you type and nothing is saved.

Is Copilot a language model here?

In this build the analysis is composed from the same result the grids show, so every figure matches the page and the same inquiry always gives the same write-up. It follows the F&SC Copilot sidecar pattern, docked beside the form.

We run standard cost, not actual.

Then the gap this page shows is your purchase price and production variance, seen before month end. Bluestem's four items are set up for batch actual costing, so each run carries the cost of the lot it consumed, and the page flags an item cost more than 5% away from what the BOM rolls up to today.

Can it live inside F&SC?

Yes. Adding ?embed=1 to the address drops the bluestem ribbon so the pages can sit in a workspace without a second header bar.

If something goes wrong

  • The page looks empty. That is the opening state. Pick an item from the lookup and press Run.
  • A red bar says "Item is a required field" or "does not exist". The Item field is blank or mistyped. Open the lookup, pick RAW-BLU or PK-BLU-PINT, and run again.
  • The numbers don't match this sheet. Someone left a filter, a simulation or the co-pack line on. Press F5; nothing is stored, so a refresh returns everything to the opening state.
  • The dark ribbon at the top is missing. The address has ?embed=1 in it. Remove that and reload.
  • The site won't load at all. Check the booth network. Once the page has loaded it needs no connection, so open it before the floor opens. Fall back to the looping video while it reconnects.
